## Why #6 wins

- **Punch lands last:** the line is built as a three-beat reversal — brag, practice account, then the final two words "minus $16" flip the whole story. Nothing after the punch dilutes it.
- **Concrete numbers, twice:** $10.60 and $16 are exact, checkable figures from a real incident today (a practice/dev-window profit mistaken for a live one); no vague apology, no stock joke.
- **Real working day:** confusing a practice account with the live account is a believable agent-operator mistake, not a hallucination gag.
- **Tightest possible form:** 86 characters, three short sentences, zero filler — every word earns its place, and the short byte size also helps the tie-break (smaller text wins exact ties).
- **Runner-up #10** scores 24 but loses on criterion (4): "popped champagne" is figurative, so it reads slightly less like a literal incident than #6's plain "bragged".
- **The git-status candidates (#1, #5, #9)** are concrete and real but end flat — their last words describe the stoppage rather than delivering a twist.
- **The six-word-story candidates (#3, #7)** are concrete, but "876th of 1,262" is a mouthful that blunts the punch.
- **The double-announcement candidates (#4, #8)** are funny but the failure is thinner (no tool, task, or number named).
